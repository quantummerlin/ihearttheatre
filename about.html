Our Journey

Nonna's Legacy

Penelope's Nonna loved the stage and the shows her whole life. As a teenager, she and her sister would go to the cinema together. When Penelope's mum came along, Nonna took her to shows too. And when Penelope arrived, Nonna carried on that tradition, taking her granddaughter along too.

The last show they saw together was an opera version of The Wizard of Oz. Penelope was very young and absolutely loved it. We had no idea how much that night would matter.

Then Nonna passed away.

The Night Everything Changed

Seven months after Nonna passed, her former work colleague Maria posted that her daughter was performing in Theatrical's production of The Wizard of Oz. We knew we had to take Penelope.

Opening night was one we'd never forget, and not just because of what happened on stage. There was flooding at the National Theatre. The foyer and main entrance were closed. Everyone had to enter through the fire escape. But the show must go on, and it did.

Penelope's seat was right in front of a makeshift red carpet and promo stand on stage. She'd come dressed as Dorothy. Pigtails, a basket with a fluffy dog inside, the whole lot. The show blew our whole family away.

On the way out, Penelope asked if she could say some words on a camera that was recording audience reactions. She did, and her clip ended up in Theatrical's highlights reel.

Maria messaged us afterwards: "The cast noticed Penelope and they want to meet her."

Days later, Penelope and a friend went back to watch the show again. She met the cast, took photos, and was welcomed by every single performer. The next day, Maria's daughter told us the executive producer had "a treat" for Penelope at interval.

So we went again, this time with another friend. At interval, the executive producer took Penelope and her friend backstage to meet the cast in full costume. Going backstage with the whole cast still in costume, that was the moment.

After the show, while waiting for photos with the remaining cast members she hadn't yet met, one of the actors (he played Dorothy's uncle and the Guard of Oz) leaned down and whispered: "Do you want to hear a secret? There's a new show coming, The Sound of Music, and they need a Gretl. You should audition."

That was it.

Becoming Gretl

Penelope got stuck into preparation. She took cheerleading classes for choreography and teamwork, trained with Sarah DiBono in local sessions, and took dancing lessons with Matilda at ELS Dance. By the time auditions came around, she was ready.

She went in with two of Nonna's favourites: "I Can't Help Falling in Love" by Elvis and "A Spoonful of Sugar". While waiting and shivering with nerves, a girl named Stephanie Stevenson, who was auditioning just before her, came over and told Penelope how beautiful she looked in her dress. That helped more than anything. Stephanie was later cast too, in one of the roles under Liesl.

The production team cheered her on. They were upfront about the workload. Three months of three-hour rehearsals, full days on weekends. Penelope got the callback invite. She was the only Gretl in attendance.

In the callback parking lot, another father looked at Penelope and said: "Well, there's a Gretl if ever I saw one."

Then came the call. Tears of joy. And a feeling we couldn't shake that Nonna had something to do with it.

The Sound of Music Season

Three months of rehearsals followed. Long sessions, full days, learning every part of how a production works. There were two casts, and Penelope was part of one, but she attended every single show. She helped sell programs, getting into every part of it.

It was a really talented cast, and the families around them had done this before. They welcomed us in. Through selling programs and watching a whole season of performances, we began to understand how every little thing affects an audience, and how the audience in turn affects those on stage.

That understanding of the relationship between performer and audience is what drives everything we do at iHeartTheatre.

Our Philosophy

Having experienced the love from an audience through The Sound of Music, we knew what these people were experiencing. We know what it's like to be the family of a kid performer on opening night. When we see the other "star" on an opening night show we're attending, we try to acknowledge them by asking for a photo with them & Pea.

It can feel weird where you are with a big crowd who are seeing a show you are in, but that night you are almost anonymous. But they are a star in their own right.

The Theatre Renaissance

We believe we are stepping into a world where AI has had such a profound effect on digital entertainment that the future is very much LIVE performance. Humanity craves something real. Real emotion, real stories.

As the love from the audience grows, so will the performance for those on stage. The energy and safety of knowing that everyone there is willing them to be fantastic transforms the experience.

As an audience, we have a duty of care to be as positive and excited as we can, in our part of the performance, to get the most of what we are seeing in front of us.

We've seen many times how a misstep or break in concentration can shake a performer. But in that moment of recovery, when they can be unsure of themselves for a moment, a cheer of encouragement can see the performer thrive.

The Audience's Role

Through selling programs and watching a whole season of performances as we completely immersed ourselves into every experience of it, we could tell how little things could affect the audience, which in turn affects those on stage.

It starts before they even get to the venue. If the journey is a hassle, or something happens on the way or just before, if the venue is hard to find or no parking, the energy can start to shift.

The second they walk through the door, we are in charge. We can control that energy between the door and the threshold to the auditorium.

How We Celebrate

We are celebrators, not critics.

Our write-ups aim to show the positives of every show, knowing that it is possible to not enjoy a performance and still appreciate the art, the effort, the production, the dedication, and still find something wonderful in it.

We write about school shows, community groups, holiday programmes, all shows. We are building a bridge of connections across Victoria in all aspects of live performance.

Love for Theatre

The Melbourne Theatre Experience

We've experienced theatre in so many different types of spaces across Melbourne:

  • Botanical Gardens - Open air, actual stars overhead
  • St Kilda Beach - A taped-off section of sand that somehow becomes a stage
  • The Malthouse - Home to some of Melbourne's most adventurous shows
  • The Round - Theatre in the round, you're practically part of it
  • Collingwood Pubs - 20 seats in an upstairs room, nothing quite like it
  • School Theatres - A special mention for Haileybury, whose facilities are simply world-class
  • National Theatre - Our second home, where you'll regularly find us volunteering to sell programs and checking in with the wonderful staff who have always shown so much love for Pea

Or we are just really lucky to live in this city with so many incredible performers and real people.
