Audition Advice

Pick a song from a similar genre and era to the show — but not from the show itself. Show range while staying comfortably in your voice. 16–32 bars for most auditions. Use the Song Browser to find something that suits you perfectly.
Dress professionally but comfortably. Think 'smart casual that you can move in'. Avoid logos or strong character costumes — you want the panel to see YOU, not your clothes. Comfortable shoes you can dance in.
Know your piece inside-out — not just the notes but the character's journey. Bring sheet music for the accompanist with your key marked. Arrive 10–15 minutes early. Introduce yourself clearly and project your name with the same confidence as your opening line.
Community theatre genuinely welcomes newcomers. Most panels want to see confidence and commitment more than perfection. Don't apologise before you start. If you make a mistake, carry on — panels watch how you recover as much as how you perform.
Breathe deeply before you start. Connect to the character and the story rather than thinking about technique — story engagement naturally improves your vocal performance. Warm up properly before you arrive.
A brief, polite thank-you email is always appropriate. If you don't get cast, ask if there's feedback available — most directors are happy to help you improve for next time. Every audition is practice.
