Lead

Annie

from Annie — Charles Strouse
soprano beltC4-Bb5Belt required (G4-Bb5)Age 8–16
One of the most iconic child roles in all of musical theatre. Annie is indestructible optimism in the face of genuine hardship — she has had a terrible life and she is completely, unshakeably certain that it will get better. 'Tomorrow' is the most famous song about hope in the commercial musical theatre canon.
Bright, clear belt soprano — 'Tomorrow' must feel genuinely confident, not wistful. The voice should sound like someone who has decided to believe. Must be bright, warm, and real — not the performed brightness of a competition singer.

What Directors Love to See
  • 'Tomorrow' — the belt must be genuine and the optimism must be completely real
  • Natural stage presence and movement confidence
  • The emotional intelligence to make the hope feel chosen, not assumed
Audition Tips
  • Tomorrow is widely known — show something specific and real in your interpretation
  • The belt should feel effortless — Annie doesn't perform, she just sings
  • Don't play Annie as cute — she's practical, resourceful, and genuinely brave
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Playing the cuteness rather than the character
  • A warbly or wavering belt — 'Tomorrow' requires a strong, clear tone
  • Making Annie too precious — she's a scrappy kid, not a china doll
