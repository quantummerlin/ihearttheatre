Lead

The Witch

from Into the Woods — Stephen Sondheim
mezzo soprano powerhouseG3-F#5Belt required (D4-F#5)Age 30–55
The most vocally and dramatically demanding role in the show. The Witch is a complex figure who cursed the Baker's family, imprisoned Rapunzel out of overprotective love, and ultimately serves as the moral truth-teller of the story. She begins as a grotesque hag, transforms into a beautiful enchantress, and eventually loses her powers when she loses what she loves most. The Witch is the only character who consistently tells the truth — and she's punished for it. Her rage, grief, and love for Rapunzel are the most operatically intense moments in the score. Bernadette Peters originated the role; it has since become a benchmark for powerhouse musical theatre actresses.
The most vocally demanding role Sondheim ever wrote. Requires legitimate legit voice for 'Stay With Me', devastating belt for 'Last Midnight', comedic character voice for Act One hag scenes, and the emotional control to navigate 'Children Will Listen'. Must handle extreme dynamic range — whisper to scream — within single phrases.

The Witch is the show's anchor — mezzo-soprano with genuine power to G5 combined with verbal precision. She is simultaneously right and wrong and she knows it. Never play her as a villain.
Avoid: Panto witch clichés — the Witch's power is ambiguity and intelligence, not evil
What Directors Love to See
  • A voice that can shake the walls AND break your heart
  • Fearless emotional commitment — the Witch holds nothing back
  • Physical transformation capability — hag to beauty convincingly
  • The intelligence to navigate Sondheim's most complex score
  • Truth — the Witch must feel like the most honest person in the room
Audition Tips
  • Prepare 'Last Midnight' or 'Stay With Me' — they show exactly what's needed
  • If not Sondheim, choose something that shows extreme vocal and emotional range
  • Study both Bernadette Peters and Donna Murphy interpretations for different valid approaches
  • Show directors you can be terrifying and tender — that's the whole role
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • All power, no vulnerability — the Witch's love for Rapunzel must be real
  • Playing the hag as cartoonish instead of genuinely threatening
  • Insufficient vocal preparation — this score demands Olympic-level singing
  • Missing the comedy in Act One — the Witch is darkly hilarious
