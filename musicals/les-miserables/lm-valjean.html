Lead

Jean Valjean

from Les Misérables — Claude-Michel Schönberg
heroic tenorD2-Bb4Age 35–60
Valjean is one of the great heroic roles in musical theatre — a former convict who rebuilds his soul through acts of compassion and sacrifice, carrying the entire show dramatically and vocally across decades of stage time. The role ages from bitter ex-prisoner to saintly surrogate father and demands a performer of the highest calibre. 'Bring Him Home' is one of the most technically demanding tenor solos ever written.
Requires sustained, pure head voice for Bring Him Home (quietly, on a high A/Bb). Also needs dramatic chest voice for Confrontation and Who Am I. A rare combination of lyric beauty and dramatic power. This is not a role for voices still developing.

What Directors Love to See
  • Bring Him Home — the quiet, sustained falsetto/head voice on a high A/Bb is the defining challenge
  • Physical and emotional stamina — Valjean is on stage for most of a 3-hour show
  • The arc from bitterness to transcendent peace must be fully plotted and genuinely felt
  • Acting depth that matches the vocal power — the singing won't win it alone
Audition Tips
  • Prepare both 'Bring Him Home' AND 'Who Am I?' — directors need to see the full range
  • Book a vocal coach specialising in mixed voice well in advance — the falsetto demands are extreme
  • Study the physical arc as closely as the vocal one — how does Valjean's body change across 30 years?
  • Listen to Alfie Boe and Philip Quast recordings for two widely different but valid approaches
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Belting 'Bring Him Home' rather than floating it in head voice — the softness is what breaks hearts
  • Playing Valjean as saintly from the start — the early bitterness makes the redemption meaningful
  • Underestimating the stamina required for the full show
