Glinda

from Wicked — Stephen Schwartz
soprano coloraturaC4-B5Age 18–35
Glinda is one of musical theatre's great comedic soprano roles — superficial, self-absorbed, and laugh-out-loud funny in Act One, before revealing unexpected depth and genuine love for Elphaba in Act Two. The role demands perfect comic timing, a bright coloratura soprano, and the ability to make the audience love someone who initially seems vacuous.
Bright, clear coloratura soprano with excellent diction for comedy. 'Popular' requires perfect comic timing within the vocal line. Head voice runs and sustained high notes are essential.

What Directors Love to See
  • Natural comedy — can you get a laugh without mugging?
  • A bright, clean soprano that handles coloratura cleanly
  • The emotional transition in Act 2 — when Glinda grieves for Elphaba, it must surprise and move us
  • Distinctive physical comedy — Glinda's movement vocabulary is as important as her voice
Audition Tips
  • 'Popular' is the obvious audition piece — if you're allowed to sing it, play the comedy completely straight
  • Show your Act 2 depth to directors — mention you've thought about where Glinda's real feelings live
  • Glinda's sadness must feel authentic, not performed — the final scenes require genuine emotion
  • Work on the high notes — the soprano demands are real, not cosmetic
Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Playing the comedy too broadly — Glinda thinks she's being sincere, which is why she's funny
  • Showing the real Glinda too early — the transformation in Act 2 needs to be a genuine revelation
  • Missing the genuine love for Elphaba — it has to be real from the start, just hidden
