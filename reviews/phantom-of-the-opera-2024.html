The Phantom of the Opera

Deanna · May 2025 · UK Tour

There are productions that remind you why you fell in love with musical theatre in the first place. Cameron Mackintosh's spectacular new production of The Phantom of the Opera is one of those productions. This is not a revival — it's a reinvention, and it is breathtaking.

From the moment the chandelier begins its ascent in the opening sequence, you know you are in the hands of a creative team operating at the very peak of their craft. The staging is sumptuous — not merely impressive but genuinely overwhelming. The scale of the production is matched only by the intimacy of its emotional core.

The Leads

The performance at the centre of this production is the kind you tell people about for years. The Phantom is played with extraordinary physical and vocal commitment — the range required for this role is enormous, and here it is delivered with what feels like effortless authority. There are moments in "The Music of the Night" where time genuinely seems to stop.

Christine is warmly human and vocally magnificent. The soprano required for this role is daunting, and there is no hedging here — every note is taken full-voiced, every emotion fully inhabited. Her "Think of Me" in the first act is one of the finest pieces of musical theatre singing I have heard in a long time.

"The chandelier — that iconic chandelier — performs its trick exactly as you hope it will, and the audience, primed by decades of expectation, gasps in exactly the right way."
— Deanna, iHeartTheatre

Production Design

The sets are a masterclass in theatrical spectacle. Every scene transition is a small miracle of engineering, and yet the production never loses sight of story in favour of show. The Phantom's lair beneath the opera house is genuinely eerie — lit with shadows and reflected candlelight that makes the auditorium feel as though it too has been transported below ground.

The costumes are extraordinary — the masquerade scene in Act II is an explosion of colour and invention that would not look out of place on the grandest stages in the world.

A Final Note

After more than three decades, The Phantom of the Opera remains one of the great works of musical theatre. This production understands that completely. It does not coast on the show's reputation — it earns it, every night, with performances and production values that are simply first-class.

If you can get tickets, go. Take tissues. You will need them.
